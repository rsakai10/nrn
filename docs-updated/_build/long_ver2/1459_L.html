Action Potential Amplitude Variability in Single Compartment Models

When simulating action potentials (APs) in single compartment models with Hodgkin-Huxley type channels (hh), the amplitude of APs can vary significantly depending on model parameters such as geometry and channel densities. It is a common misconception that the AP amplitude must remain constant both during a spike train and across different parameter sets. However, the model’s ionic current balances fundamentally determine the AP peak voltage.

Explanation of AP Peak Voltage

For a single electrically compact compartment, membrane voltage dynamics satisfy the equation:

\[C \frac{dV}{dt} = \sum_x I_x\]

where \(C\) is the membrane capacitance and \(I_x\) are ionic currents for species \(x\). At the peak of an AP, \(dV/dt = 0\), so the sum of ionic currents is zero:

\[0 = \sum_x g_x (V - E_x)\]

where \(g_x\) and \(E_x\) are the conductance and reversal potential of ionic species \(x\), respectively. Rearranging:

\[V = \frac{\sum_x g_x E_x}{\sum_x g_x}\]

This means the AP peak voltage is a conductance-weighted average of ionic reversal potentials at the spike peak. The relative magnitudes of sodium, potassium, and leak conductances at the peak determine the AP amplitude.

Consequently:

  • An increase in potassium conductance (\(g_K\)) or a decrease in sodium conductance (\(g_{Na}\)) at the spike peak reduces AP amplitude.

  • Channel gating dynamics during repetitive spiking, such as sodium inactivation and potassium activation, can reduce the maximum AP amplitude across spikes.

Parameter Effects on Single Compartment Models

Given a single compartment model created as:

create A
A {
  L = 10
  diam = 10
  insert hh
}

and injecting current clamp (IClamp) with parameters such as del=200, dur=100, and amp=0.02, different spike counts and amplitudes are observed.

If the compartment diameter is decreased (e.g., A.diam=1), the input resistance increases, causing more spikes to fire. However, the first spike amplitude can remain large (~42 mV peak), but subsequent spikes may have drastically smaller amplitudes (near 0 mV).

Adjusting maximal conductances (e.g., \(\bar{g}_{Na}\), \(\bar{g}_{K}\)) changes excitability and amplitude. For example:

  • Increasing sodium conductance (gnabar_hh()) increases excitability and can cause spontaneous firing.

  • The leak conductance generally has little impact on AP amplitude.

  • Changes in membrane capacitance affect the speed but less so the amplitude of spikes.

Strategies to Attain Consistent AP Amplitudes

To generate APs with similar amplitudes across cells or compartments of different sizes:

  1. Adjust maximal ionic conductance densities (\(\bar{g}_{Na}\), \(\bar{g}_{K}\)) rather than absolute conductances.

  2. Small compartments typically require higher channel densities to achieve the same ionic currents and thus similar AP amplitudes.

  3. Use tools such as the Multiple Run Fitter to automatically tune parameters to achieve desired electrophysiological behavior.

  4. Consider the presence of additional compartments or branches which may act as electrical loads reducing apparent amplitude.

Code Example: Python and HOC

Here is a simple example creating a single compartment model with Hodgkin-Huxley channels, applying current clamp, and observing APs:

create soma
soma {
  L = 10
  diam = 10
  insert hh
}
objref stim
stim = new IClamp(0.5)
stim.del = 200
stim.dur = 100
stim.amp = 0.02

proc run_sim() {
    tstop = 400
    run()
}

from neuron import h, gui

soma = h.Section(name='soma')
soma.L = 10
soma.diam = 10
soma.insert('hh')

stim = h.IClamp(soma(0.5))
stim.delay = 200
stim.dur = 100
stim.amp = 0.02

h.tstop = 400
h.run()

Notes

  • The maximum voltage during a spike depends on the balance of ionic conductances and their gating variables at spike peak.

  • During repetitive firing, sodium channel inactivation and potassium channel activation lead to reduced AP amplitude.

  • Cell size scaling requires adjusting ion channel densities for consistent electrophysiological properties.

  • Leak channels contribute less to spike amplitude, but important for resting membrane potential.

  • For complex morphologies with dendritic branches, passive or active loads may modify soma spike amplitude.

Note

Monitoring gating variables during simulation can provide insight into changes in conductances underlying AP amplitude variations.
