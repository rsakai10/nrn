E_pas and Setting Resting Potential in NEURON Models

When inserting the passive membrane mechanism (pas) into a Section, it is important to understand the roles of its parameters, especially g_pas and e_pas.

Passive Parameters

  • The membrane resistance per unit area (Rm) is commonly reported in units of ohm·cm².

  • In NEURON, the passive conductance (g_pas) is the inverse of Rm:

# Given Rm from literature in ohm·cm²
Rm = 20000  # example value
sec.insert('pas')
sec.g_pas = 1.0 / Rm  # siemens/cm²

  • The membrane capacitance per unit area (cm) is also set based on literature or experimental data:

sec.cm = 1.0  # typical value in uF/cm²

Meaning and Use of e_pas

  • e_pas is the reversal (equilibrium) potential of the leak current mediated by the passive mechanism.

  • Conceptually, e_pas reflects the biophysical property of the passive conductance.

  • It is not an arbitrary initial condition, but a parameter dictating the “resting” voltage that passive currents tend to drive the membrane toward.

Setting Initial Voltage (v_init) in Relation to e_pas

  • v_init is the initial membrane potential at the start of a simulation.

  • If a section has only the passive mechanism (pas) inserted, its resting membrane potential is determined by e_pas.

  • To start the simulation close to the resting state and avoid transients, set:

h.v_init = sec.e_pas

  • Setting e_pas equal to v_init is a common practice in simple passive models.

More Complex Models with Multiple Ion Channels and Compartments

  • In cells with active conductances in the soma (channels, pumps, exchangers) and passive dendrites:

    • The resting potential of the cell is generally determined by the ensemble of all ionic currents.

    • Each section’s e_pas in passive dendrites should ideally reflect the actual equilibrium potential for the leak currents of that compartment.

    • Assigning e_pas to the overall cell resting potential (e.g., -65 mV) in passive sections can be reasonable if you conceptualize the passive dendrites as contributing this resting conductance.

  • When applying voltage clamp or holding the neuron at an imposed potential (e.g., -100 mV):

    • The value of e_pas does not necessarily change.

    • Voltage clamp sets membrane voltage externally, overriding currents.

    • e_pas should remain consistent with the biological leakage reversal potential rather than being set equal to the clamp potential.

Initialization and Model Dynamics

  • NEURON’s default initialization simulates clamping the membrane at v_init until steady state is reached, then releases the clamp at t=0.

  • If ionic mechanisms and conductances are spatially nonuniform, the membrane potential may drift after initialization due to heterogeneous driving forces.

  • Ion concentration dynamics complicate initial steady state and often require extended simulations or specialized initialization routines (e.g., as discussed in advanced NEURON documentation).

  • Users should plan simulation initialization according to the model’s complexity and goals:

    1. For simple passive models, setting v_init = e_pas ensures initial resting state.

    2. For complex models with multiple conductances and ion dynamics, allow for a pre-stimulus settling period or use explicit steady-state initialization methods.

Summary

  • Set g_pas = 1 / Rm and cm according to literature values.

  • Set e_pas to the biologically realistic equilibrium potential of the leak current.

  • In purely passive models, initialize v_init to e_pas to start at resting potential.

  • In complex models, interpret e_pas as a biophysical parameter rather than an initial voltage; handle initialization carefully to reach physiological steady state before stimulation.

Example Usage in Python

from neuron import h

sec = h.Section(name='dendrite')
sec.L = 100  # length in microns
sec.diam = 1  # diameter in microns
sec.insert('pas')

Rm = 20000  # ohm·cm² from literature
Cm = 1.0    # uF/cm² typical membrane capacitance

sec.g_pas = 1.0 / Rm  # S/cm²
sec.cm = Cm
sec.e_pas = -65       # mV, leak reversal potential from experimental data

h.v_init = sec.e_pas  # start simulation near resting membrane potential

Example Usage in HOC

create dendrite
dendrite {
    L = 100
    diam = 1
    insert pas
    g_pas = 1/(20000)  // S/cm²
    cm = 1
    e_pas = -65         // mV
}
v_init = -65            // mV

Note

Always align your model parameters and initialization strategy with the conceptual and experimental framework of your study. Proper initialization is critical to avoid artificial dynamics at simulation start.
