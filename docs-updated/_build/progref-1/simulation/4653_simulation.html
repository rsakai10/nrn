Applying Sinusoidal Voltage to Membrane

When intending to apply a sinusoidal voltage to a model axon, it is important to clarify whether the goal is to force the membrane potential or the extracellular potential to follow a sinusoidal time course.

  • To force membrane potential to follow a sinusoidal waveform, the SEClamp is the appropriate tool, as it clamps the membrane potential to a specified waveform by injecting current on a per-segment basis. The older VClamp is not recommended because it was designed for experimental squid axon voltage clamp studies.

  • Injecting a sinusoidal current via an IClamp will not produce a sinusoidal membrane potential due to ionic channel dynamics.

  • For forcing the extracellular potential to follow a time-varying waveform (e.g., sinusoidal), one should not use clamps but rather modify the extracellular potential directly.

A recommended approach for modeling time-varying extracellular stimulation in a purely resistive, linear extracellular medium is to use the xstim density mechanism, which drives e_extracellular through a POINTER variable linked to a global stimulus waveform.

forall {
    insert xstim
    for (x=0) setpointer ex_xstim(x), e_extracellular(x)
}

Here, the global variable controlling stimulation amplitude (e.g. is_xstim) is driven externally by a function generator (like Fsq or Fzap) or by Vector.play(). The parameter rx is set per segment based on the maximum local extracellular potential predicted by your COMSOL or other modeling tools, converted to mV:

rx = 1e-3 * (maximum local extracellular potential in Volts)

This ensures the spatial and temporal profile of extracellular potential corresponds to your input waveform scaled appropriately per segment.

Note

The extracellular potential waveform at any position p relates linearly to the input current stimulus waveform i(t):

\[v_{ext}(p, t) = r(p) \times i(t)\]

where \(r(p)\) is a location-specific resistance factor.

With this method, the time course of extracellular potential at all axon segments can be controlled precisely according to experimental or modeling data, allowing comparisons such as those inspired by Gaines et al. (2018). Original Thread: https://neuron.yale.edu/phpBB/viewtopic.php?t=4653
