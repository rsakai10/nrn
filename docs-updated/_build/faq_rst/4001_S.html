Difference Between Weight and Reversal Potential for Synaptic Connections

When using NetCon with conductance-based synapses like ExpSyn or Exp2Syn, the reversal potential (e.g. .e) and the weight parameter fulfill distinct roles:

  • The reversal potential .e sets the driving force direction and the ion type of the synaptic current.

  • The weight parameter of NetCon scales the amplitude of the synaptic conductance transient triggered by a presynaptic spike.

The weight should be a non-negative value representing the peak conductance change (in µS). The sign and magnitude of the postsynaptic current arise from the difference between the membrane voltage and the reversal potential. Thus, inhibitory effects typically have reversal potentials negative relative to resting potential, with positive weights.

For example, in HOC:

objref GABA
soma {
    GABA = new Exp2Syn(0.5)
    GABA.tau1 = 30
    GABA.tau2 = 100
    GABA.e = -75  // inhibitory reversal potential
}
NetCon(&v(0.5), GABA, -40, 1, 0.5)  // weight = 0.5 (peak conductance, always positive)

Attempting to use a negative weight to invert current direction (e.g., weight = -1 and e = +75 vs. weight = +1 and e = -75) is incorrect, as synaptic weights represent conductances and cannot be negative without violating physical principles.
