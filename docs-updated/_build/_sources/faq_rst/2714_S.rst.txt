.. rubric:: Single Compartment Neuron Models: Usage and Considerations

Single compartment models approximate neurons as a single electrically uniform segment with parameters such as soma diameter and surface area. These models are especially useful for:

- Testing individual ionic channel mechanisms (:class:`Channel`, :mod:`NMODL`).
- Exploring membrane and intracellular dynamics without spatial complexity.
- Simplified modeling of small or electrotonically compact cells or networks where detailed morphology is less critical.

**Model construction tips:**

- Base surface area on experimental measurements or estimates of membrane capacitance (:attr:`Cm`) and input resistance (:attr:`Ra`), assuming specific membrane capacitance ≈ 1 µF/cm².
- Insert ionic conductances using :file:`.mod` files and define maximum conductances (`gbar`) to fit voltage-clamp data.
- Total transmembrane currents recorded as densities (mA/cm²) can be converted to absolute currents (mA) by multiplying by surface area, e.g.:

  .. code-block:: python

     # Assuming section 'soma' with nseg=1 and recorded vector inavec for sodium current density
     absolute_current = inavec.mul(0.01 * soma(0.5).area())

**Regarding stimulation and recording:**

- Current clamp (:class:`IClamp`) and voltage clamp (:class:`SEClamp`) simulate intracellular manipulations, not extracellular stimulation.
- Extracellular stimulation applies current outside the membrane and requires explicit extracellular mechanisms.
- In single compartment models (nseg=1), spatial distinctions like stimulating or recording at different segment locations are not meaningful—voltage is uniform.
- Dual recordings or injecting current and recording at different sites require multicompartment models or multiple connected cells (e.g., coupled by gap junctions).

**Modeling gap junctions:**

- Represent electrically coupled cells as separate sections connected by resistive junctions.
- Use multiple sections or cells in NEURON to simulate intercellular current flow and voltage changes, which cannot be captured in a simple single compartment.

.. note::

Single compartment models are simplifications and cannot capture spatially distributed channel dynamics or dendritic processing. Start simple and add complexity only if necessary to address your scientific question.
