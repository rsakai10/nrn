Difference Between Synaptic Weight and Reversal Potential in NEURON
=======================================================================

When creating synaptic connections in NEURON using classes such as :class:`ExpSyn` or :class:`Exp2Syn`, it is important to understand the distinct roles of the synaptic **weight** and the synaptic **reversal potential** (:attr:`.e`).

Synaptic Weight
--------------

- The **weight** parameter in the :class:`NetCon` object determines the *magnitude* of the conductance change elicited by a presynaptic spike on the synaptic point process.
- The weight corresponds to the *peak conductance* (in µS) introduced by a single presynaptic spike.
- This parameter must be **non-negative** when connecting to biophysical conductance-based synaptic mechanisms like :class:`ExpSyn` or :class:`Exp2Syn`.
- The synaptic weight does *not* carry sign information about excitation or inhibition; it only scales the size of the conductance transient.
- Negative weights are appropriate only in artificial integrate-and-fire type models (e.g., :class:`IntFire1`) but **not** when modeling conductance changes in biophysical cells.

Reversal Potential (:attr:`.e`)
-------------------------------

- The reversal potential (:attr:`.e`) of a synapse defines the *driving force* for the synaptic current.
- It represents the equilibrium membrane potential for the ion channels opened by the synapse.
- The sign and magnitude of synaptic current depend on the difference between the membrane potential and reversal potential:  
  \[
  I_{syn} = g_{syn}(t) \times (V_m - E_{rev})
  \]
- For inhibitory synapses (e.g., GABAergic synapses), a typical reversal potential is negative relative to the resting potential (e.g., \(-75\ mV\)).
- For excitatory synapses (e.g., AMPA), reversal potentials are usually positive (e.g., \(0\ mV\)).

Relationship Between Weight and Reversal Potential
--------------------------------------------------

- Setting **weight = 1** and **e = -75 mV** is *not* equivalent to **weight = -1** and **e = 75 mV**.
- Negative weights are not valid for conductance-based synapses because the synaptic current direction depends on the reversal potential.
- Instead, the sign (inhibitory vs. excitatory) is encoded in the reversal potential.
- The weight modulates how much conductance is introduced; the reversal potential determines current direction and nature (excitatory vs. inhibitory).

Example in HOC
--------------

```hoc
objref GABA
OLMCells[0].soma {
    GABA = new Exp2Syn(0.5)
    GABA.tau1 = 30
    GABA.tau2 = 100
    GABA.e = -75   // Inhibitory reversal potential
}

// Connecting with NetCon
OLMCells[0].soma {
    // NetCon targeting the GABA synapse
    // Weight of 0.5 µS, threshold of -40 mV
    nclist.append(new NetCon(&v(0.5), GABA, -40, 1, 0.5))
}
```

Example in Python
-----------------

```python
from neuron import h

gaba = h.Exp2Syn(0.5)
gaba.tau1 = 30
gaba.tau2 = 100
gaba.e = -75  # inhibitory reversal potential

netcon = h.NetCon(h.soma(0.5)._ref_v, gaba, sec=h.soma)
netcon.threshold = -40
netcon.weight[0] = 0.5  # peak conductance in µS
```

.. note::
   - The **synaptic weight** should always be non-negative for conductance-based synapses.
   - The **sign of the synaptic effect (excitatory vs. inhibitory)** is determined by the reversal potential (:attr:`.e`) relative to the membrane potential, *not* by the weight.
   - Negative weights for conductance-based synapses are not physically meaningful and violate thermodynamic principles.
