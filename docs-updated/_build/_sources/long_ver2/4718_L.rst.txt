Multicompartment Soma
======================

When modeling neurons with realistic morphologies and conductance distributions, special considerations arise regarding how to represent the soma compartment and how to handle multiple inhibitory synapses in simulations.

Modeling a Multicompartment Soma
-------------------------------

- **When to consider a multicompartment soma**

  For electrical signaling, somatic compartments in vertebrates are typically modeled as a single compartment because the soma is small enough that voltage equilibrates rapidly and nearly uniformly over its extent.

  However, if your study focuses on spatially localized chemical dynamics within the soma, such as calcium signaling, buffering, or interactions with organelles (e.g., smooth endoplasmic reticulum, mitochondria), then spatial discretization of the soma into multiple compartments may be necessary. This can be implemented using:

  - Custom NMODL mechanisms with explicitly defined intracellular diffusion
  - The :mod:`rxd` module for reaction-diffusion modeling of ions and molecules

- **Estimating somatic conductivity**

  Determining accurate cytoplasmic resistivity and the electrical properties of organelles (like the nucleus) is challenging.

  A common physics-based modeling approach approximates the soma as a spherical compartment attached to a cylindrical dendrite (ball-and-stick model). This morphology can be represented using :meth:`hoc.Section.pt3dadd` with multiple 3D points (e.g., 21 or more) to approximate a sphere. Empirically-based models of mammalian pyramidal neurons from the literature (e.g., hippocampal CA1 neurons) provide biophysical size and membrane properties.

  To explore the influence of somatic structures:

  1. Insert a synapse (e.g., :class:`AlphaSynapse`) at the soma edge opposite the dendrite.
  2. Adjust the synaptic peak conductance to produce a desired voltage deflection (e.g., 1 mV depolarization).
  3. Measure voltages along the dendrite at different normalized positions (`loc=0`, `0.5`, and `1`) to establish control voltage spread.
  4. Consider adding an insulating spherical nucleus inside the soma to test altered conductivity (implementation details require advanced modeling techniques).

Modeling Multiple Inhibitory Synapses on the Soma
-------------------------------------------------

- **Single compartment soma with multiple synapses**

  It is common and reasonable to attach multiple inhibitory synapses to the same somatic compartment.

- **Implementation approaches**

  - Use multiple instances of synaptic mechanisms such as :class:`AlphaSynapse` or :class:`Exp2Syn` all connected to the soma section.
  
  - If all synapses have identical activation timing, a single synapse with scaled peak conductance (or scaled weight in the case of :class:`NetStim`) can represent multiple synapses.
  
  - For synapses activated at different times, instantiate one synapse per event timing:
  
    ```python
    from neuron import h

    soma = h.Section(name='soma')
    synapses = []
    stimuli = []
    times = [5, 10, 15]  # example activation times in ms

    for t in times:
        syn = h.AlphaSynapse(soma(0.5))
        syn.e = -80  # inhibitory reversal potential in mV
        syn.onset = t
        syn.tau = 3  # time constant in ms
        syn.gmax = 0.001  # peak conductance in uS
        synapses.append(syn)
    ```

  - Alternatively, use :class:`NetStim` to generate spike trains and connect them to synapses for additional timing control.

Example HOC snippet:

```hoc
create soma
access soma
for i=0,2 {
   objref syn[i]
   syn[i] = new AlphaSynapse(0.5)
   syn[i].e = -80
   syn[i].tau = 3
   // Schedule synaptic events at different times
   NetCon nc
   nc = new NetCon(&t(0), syn[i])
   nc.delay = 5*(i+1)
   nc.weight = 0.001
}
```

Summary
-------

- Electrical signaling in typical vertebrate somas can be modeled using a single compartment.

- Multicompartment models may be justified for modeling spatially detailed chemical dynamics.

- Multiple inhibitory synapses can be attached to the same compartment; scale synapse strength or instantiate multiple synapse objects depending on activation patterns.

- Geometrical and biophysical parameters can be approximated using ball-and-stick morphology and known empirical data to estimate effective somatic conductivity.

.. note::

   If your modeling requires incorporating complex intracellular structures such as insulating nuclei or localized biochemical processes within the soma, investigate NEURON's :mod:`rxd` reaction-diffusion module or develop custom NMODL mechanisms capable of representing spatial heterogeneity.
